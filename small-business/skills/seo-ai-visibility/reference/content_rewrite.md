# Content Rewrite

How to write pages that read well to a customer and quote accurately when a machine reads them. These are the same thing more often than the SEO industry admits.

Read [the shared voice profile](../../../shared/voice-profile.md) before writing anything in the owner's name. A page that sounds like a marketing agency wrote it is the exact thing the owner is trying to avoid.

---

## The structure

1. **Answer the question in the first two sentences.** Whatever the page is about, say it immediately. Machines quote openings; people read them and leave if the answer is not there.
2. **Then the specifics.** Prices, timelines, what is included, what is not, where you work.
3. **Then the context.** Why it matters, how the work is done, what to expect.
4. **One clear next step.** Call, book, ask.

Most small-business pages do this backwards — three paragraphs of company history, then the answer, then a phone number in the footer.

---

## What makes a passage quotable

| Do | Instead of |
|---|---|
| "Tune-ups start at USD 89 and take about an hour." | "Competitive pricing on our comprehensive service." |
| "We work in Hackensack, Paramus, Teaneck, Englewood, and Fort Lee." | "Proudly serving the tri-state area." |
| "Emergency service, 7 days, usually on site within 2 hours." | "Fast, reliable response when you need us most." |
| "Family-owned since 2009. NJ HVACR license 19HC00123400." | "Decades of trusted experience." |

The pattern: a specific, checkable fact in a complete sentence. Adjectives are unquotable because they say nothing a machine can verify or a customer can act on.

## Titles and meta descriptions

**Title** — under about 60 characters, unique, says what the page is, leads with the thing being searched for.

```
Bad:   Home | Okonkwo Mechanical | Your Trusted HVAC Partner Since 2009
Good:  Commercial HVAC Service in Bergen County NJ | Okonkwo Mechanical
```

**Meta description** — under about 155 characters, reads like a person, contains a reason to click.

```
Bad:   Okonkwo Mechanical provides HVAC solutions to residential and
       commercial customers with quality service and competitive pricing.
Good:  HVAC repair, installation, and maintenance across Bergen County.
       Tune-ups from USD 89. Emergency service 7 days. Family-owned since 2009.
```

## Question pages

Customers ask questions out loud now, so a page organized around real questions performs on both sides.

Use questions the owner actually gets. Pull them from email, the inbox, quote requests, and reviews. Invented FAQs read like invented FAQs.

```
## How much does an AC tune-up cost?

USD 89 for a standard residential system. That covers a full system check,
coil cleaning, refrigerant level, and a written report on anything that
looks likely to fail. Takes about an hour. Second system at the same
address is USD 60.

## How fast can you get here in an emergency?

Usually within 2 hours during business hours, same day outside them. If
we can't make it in that window we'll say so on the phone rather than
leave you waiting.
```

Short answer first, then the detail. Then mark the section up as `FAQPage` schema, because the content genuinely is a FAQ.

## Location pages

Real local pages, or none. Ten pages with the town name swapped are duplicate content — search engines discount them and assistants learn nothing.

A location page earns its existence with something true and specific to that place: the buildings served there, the response time from the shop, a local job, a permit or code particular to the town.

If there is nothing specific to say about a town, list it on the main service-area page instead. That is a better outcome than a thin page.

## Where the honesty rule bites

**Never write a fact the owner has not confirmed.** Rewriting is not inventing. Specific detail is what makes the page work, which creates constant pressure to fill gaps with plausible numbers.

The move is to ask, in a batch, once:

```
To write these pages properly I need six things confirmed:

1. Standard tune-up price — the site says USD 89, is that current?
2. Emergency response time you'll actually stand behind
3. Which towns you'll drive to, and which you won't
4. License number as it should appear
5. Year founded — the About page says 2009, the footer says 2011
6. Saturday hours — the site and your Google listing disagree
```

Then write. Leave a visible gap rather than a guess where an answer does not come back: "PRICE TO CONFIRM" is a fine thing to hand an owner. A made-up price on a live page is not.

---

## Length

There is no target word count. Write until the question is answered, then stop.

Thin pages fail because they do not answer anything, not because they are short. Padding a 200-word page to 1,200 words with filler makes it worse for both readers and machines.
