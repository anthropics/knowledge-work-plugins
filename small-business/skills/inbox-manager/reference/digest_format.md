# Digest format

The digest is the product. Everything else this skill does is upstream of it.

**Target: ninety seconds to read.** The owner is holding coffee. If it takes longer than that, the triage was too loose, not the format too tight.

---

## Shape

1. **One-line headline** — how much came in, how much needs them.
2. **Needs you** — ranked by consequence, each with the ask in one line and what happens if it waits.
3. **Drafted and waiting** — sender, one-line summary of the reply, grouped where similar.
4. **Handed off** — one line each, naming the receiving skill *and what happens next*: where the item now sits and what the owner says to pick it up (e.g. "→ ap-processor — bill staged there; say 'process my bills' to review"). A handoff line the owner can't act on is a dead end.
5. **Covered by someone else** — threads a colleague already answered. One line each, no draft written.
6. **Handled** — a single count with categories, ending with a fixed suffix so the owner knows what "handled" means: "filed and archived — nothing needs you; ask to see any category."
7. **The approval prompt** — one question.

The draft bodies come after the digest, not inside it. In the artifact
rendering, each draft body is a copy block (the style guide's copy-button
component) so a single reply can be copied and sent by hand without
approving the batch. The digest stays skimmable; the full text of every draft follows underneath it, in the same order as the drafted list, so the owner can read any one before approving.

Needs-you always goes first. Chronological order buries the item that matters.

## The approval prompt

One question, two ways to answer. Not three.

```
Send all 11 drafts, or review them one at a time?
```

**Never send before the answer.** Drafting is automatic because it saves the hour. Sending is not, because every reply carries the owner's name and becomes a commitment they will be held to.

If the owner says "send all," send all and report what went out. If they say review, show one at a time with the original above the draft, so they can see what is being answered.

---

## When there is no connector

Same digest, different last line. The drafts are the deliverable:

```
Here are the 11 drafts, ready to copy into your mail client. Want them as
one file or one at a time?
```

That is a complete outcome, not a degraded one. The owner still skipped the hour of triage and the hour of writing.

---

## What to leave out

- **Subject lines in bulk.** A list of 63 subjects is the problem restated.
- **Explanations of how the sorting worked.** The owner cares that it is right, not how.
- **Anything in the handled bucket, itemized.** A count and categories. They can ask.
- **Confidence hedging on every item.** If you were unsure about one, say so on that one.
